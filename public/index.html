What is a keylogger?

A keylogger (keystroke logger) is surveillance technology that records each keystroke typed on a computer’s keyboard. Think of someone watching you type over your shoulder: the keys are seen before they are ever encrypted. That is why a keylogger steals passwords without breaking encryption — anything that reads the keyboard stream captures passwords and OTPs. It needs one global hook and a few dozen lines of Python.

Types

  • Software — kernel-level: operates at OS kernel level, deep access, hardest to detect.
  • Software — API-level: hooks the APIs the OS uses to receive keystroke data.
  • Software — form grabbers: capture data submitted in web forms before it is encrypted (e.g. banking sites).
  • Hardware: physical devices between keyboard and CPU (USB / PS/2), or embedded in the keyboard; wireless sniffers. Harder to detect through software, but need physical access.

Real-world incidents

Keylogging is the entry point, not the payload. Real incidents show why catching it early matters.

  • 2007–11 · Zeus — keylogging plus form grabbing to steal banking logins. Operation Trident Breach: 100+ arrests, about $70M stolen.
  • 2010 · ATM heist, Europe — a keylogger installed via USB drives helped steal credentials used to withdraw large sums from ATMs across the continent.
  • 2011–14 · GameOver Zeus — P2P variant that also spread CryptoLocker. Operation Tovar (2014): over $100M lost, 500k–1M PCs.
  • 2018–19 · HawkEye — malware-as-a-service, delivered at scale through invoice-themed phishing emails.
  • 2014–now · Agent Tesla — .NET remote access tool with keylogging and screen capture; COVID-themed phishing in 2020.
  • Corporate espionage — rogue employees have installed keyloggers to monitor executives and steal trade secrets and financial documents.

MFA and user awareness limit the damage: a stolen password alone is not enough.

Detection & countermeasures

Behavioural signs

  • Slow keyboard response or typing delays, especially if the logging buffer is poorly managed.
  • Unexplained CPU spikes when the system is idle.

Defensive tools

  • Windows Defender with updated signatures — catches known samples (flagged the packaged .exe in seconds).
  • Malwarebytes Anti-Rootkit — identifies and removes known keylogger patterns.
  • KeyScrambler — encrypts keystrokes before they reach the OS, rendering most software keyloggers useless.
  • Sysinternals Process Explorer — reveals hidden or suspicious processes in more detail than Task Manager.

Manual inspection

  • Open msconfig or taskschd.msc and inspect startup entries and scheduled tasks.
  • Use tasklist /v or Task Manager to spot unfamiliar processes, especially ones masquerading under generic names.

The legal line — India

  • IT Act s.43 — unauthorised access, civil liability.
  • IT Act s.66 — dishonest or fraudulent act, up to 3 years / ₹5 lakh.
  • IT Act s.66C — identity theft, up to 3 years / ₹1 lakh.
  • DPDP Act 2023 — personal data protection duties.
  • CERT-In — 6-hour incident reporting.

Lawful only on your own machine or with written consent. Legal and ethical uses: employee monitoring with informed consent, parental control, and ethical hacking in controlled environments. Unauthorised installation on someone else’s device is a criminal offence.

Architecture & workflow

The nine-step workflow from the summer-training report — how the simulation works, end to end.

  1. Initialization — the script starts silently as a background process (pythonw.exe, or a PyInstaller .exe with no console window).
  2. Keyboard listening — pynput.keyboard.Listener intercepts every keypress in real time, including Enter, Shift and Backspace.
  3. In-memory buffering — keystrokes sit in a list in memory; no disk I/O, nothing to find on the drive.
  4. Periodic log compilation — at fixed intervals (every 5 minutes) the buffer is compiled into a log.
  5. Secure email dispatch — the log is sent to a pre-configured address with smtplib over SMTP_SSL.
  6. Local cleanup — the local log is deleted after dispatch to minimise the forensic footprint.
  7. Persistent background execution — the logger keeps running until shutdown or manual termination.
  8. EXE conversion for stealth — PyInstaller packages it as a lightweight .exe (under 5 MB), easy to disguise as a system utility.
  9. Auto-execution on boot — startup folder entry or scheduled task relaunches it on every reboot.
